Introduction & Administrative Context

Papua New Guinea continues to rely heavily on legacy paper-based recordkeeping and centralized database systems, where administrative processing is concentrated in Port Moresby (Department of Information and Communications Technology, 2022). This structural centralization creates severe administrative delays for provincial offices across the country.

Permissionless Networks

Open public networks (e.g. Bitcoin, Ethereum) allowing unrestricted participation and anonymous node validation. Unsuitable for sovereign public administration due to privacy and speed limitations.

Permissioned Networks (Proposed)

Restricted architecture where validation rights are limited to authorized state entities (provincial capitals, police HQ, banks). Ensures data privacy, speed, and strict national sovereignty.

For PNG public administration, adopting a permissioned blockchain architecture is essential, as it restricts validation rights to trusted state entities, ensuring strict data privacy and national sovereignty while eliminating central bottlenecks.

Report Focus: Establishing a permissioned blockchain framework for digital identity as the foundational anchor, followed by dependent service domains including healthcare, criminal records, financial inclusion, and customary land registries.

2

Digital Identity as the Foundational Anchor

2.1 SevisPass Verification Bottlenecks and Centralized NID Backlogs

To modernize public service access, the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) introduced SevisPass and SevisWallet as front-end digital identity solutions. However, these platforms remain a work in progress and struggle to achieve widespread adoption because a digital credential application is fundamentally dependent on verified underlying records.

1.3M / 3.8M

The Papua New Guinea Civil and Identity Registry (PNGCIR) relies on a centralized model in Port Moresby, creating a massive backlog where only 1.3 million physical NID cards have been issued out of 3.8 million registered citizens (Tilto, 2025). Without a complete, verified data pool, front-end initiatives stall.

2.2 Permissioned Provincial Nodes: Re-engineering the Identity Anchor

To eliminate this central bottleneck, PNG can re-engineer identity verification using a permissioned blockchain architecture. Under this model, provincial registration offices operate as authorized validation nodes on a shared network.

1. Local Capture

Biometric & demographic data registered at provincial registration nodes.

2. Decentralized ID (DID)

Unique cryptographic DID generated & written directly to the ledger.

3. SevisPass Sync

Instant verification pool unlocked for SevisPass & SevisWallet apps.

Dependent Service Verification Domains

Select a key public administration sector below to examine the specific blockchain transformation:

Cross-Provincial Healthcare Records (EHR)

As reported by The National (2023), many public healthcare facilities in Papua New Guinea rely on fragmented paper records that are highly prone to loss, damage, and administrative inconsistencies. Apart from loss and damage of records, patients are being re-diagnosed and sometimes misdiagnosed when their clinic books are unavailable, wasting money and clinical supplies.

Blockchain Mechanism:

By implementing Encrypted Electronic Health Records (EHR) on a shared ledger unlocked through a patient's SevisPass ID, authorized health workers nationwide can access medical histories instantly. This seamless verification eliminates duplicate diagnostic testing and reduces medical supply waste across provincial lines.

Law Enforcement and Criminal Records (RPNGC)

In Papua New Guinea, criminal records are currently isolated within local and provincial stations, causing background checks—such as Police Clearance Certificates—to take weeks or months to process (Royal Papua New Guinea Constabulary, 2024).

Blockchain Mechanism:

Implementing a blockchain-based registry permanently links criminal records to a citizen’s verified SevisPass ID. This ensures background queries are accurate nationwide, preventing individuals from clearing their record simply by moving across provincial borders and dramatically streamlining police operations.

Banking & Financial Inclusion (KYC)

Bank of Papua New Guinea (2024) highlights that approximately 80% to 85% of the population remains unbanked. Most rural citizens are unbanked because commercial banks require strict “Know Your Customer” (KYC) requirements that citizens cannot satisfy without physical ID cards. Smallholder farmers are forced to rely on cash, leaving them vulnerable to theft and unable to access micro-loans or savings accounts.

Blockchain Mechanism:

Linking a citizen’s verified cryptographic ID—such as SevisPass—directly to financial services allows banks to verify identity instantly without requiring physical paperwork or travel to main branches. Consequently, this integration enables mobile wallets, secure crop payments, and direct access to formal banking for rural communities.

Customary Land Records & ILG Registration

Approximately 97% of PNG’s land mass is held under customary tenure, governed by clans through Incorporated Land Group (ILG) registrations (Department of Lands and Physical Planning, 2020). However, paper-based registration systems at the national level are prone to document tampering, fraudulent land transactions, and endless boundary litigation.

Blockchain Mechanism:

Before an ILG title or customary lease can be recorded, cryptographic identities of executive clan members are verified via SevisPass. Once verified, boundary agreements are immutably logged on the ledger, requiring multi-signature consensus from verified clan representatives before any transfer or lease can occur.

Implementation Constraints & Technical Feasibility

According to the World Bank (2024), only 14% of the population in PNG has access to grid electricity, with less than 4% in rural areas where frequent blackouts are experienced.

Infrastructure Workaround: Offline Caching Architecture

PNG is still a developing country, so a national digital ledger cannot rely on continuous 24/7 internet connectivity or uninterrupted electricity. The practical workaround is a permissioned network paired with local offline caching.

Remote health posts, police stations, banks, and district land offices can process and cryptographically sign records locally during power or network outages. Once power or network connectivity is restored, the local node automatically synchronizes its transactions back to the national ledger without losing any data.

5

Conclusion & Strategic Policy Roadmap

Digital identity serves as the essential foundation for modernizing Papua New Guinea’s public administration. Once current SevisPass and NID verification backlogs are resolved, dependent public and socioeconomic domains—such as healthcare, law enforcement, formal financial services, and customary land registries—can seamlessly integrate into the digital framework.

Implementing a permissioned blockchain with local offline caching addresses severe electricity and network constraints, drastically reducing provincial administrative delays. Ultimately, decentralization provides the structural architecture PNG needs to transition away from legacy paper archives and centralized database bottlenecks toward efficient, real-time public service delivery.
